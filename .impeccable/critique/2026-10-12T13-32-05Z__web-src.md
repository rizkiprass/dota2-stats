## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Strongly authored, not category-interchangeable. The ledger concept is real, not a veneer: tabular figures everywhere, a fixed azure/gold Radiant-vs-Dire semantic pair, an earned-gold rule for medals and leading values, and hero art used as spotlight/margin illustration rather than background noise. Where it leans generic (bento KPI cards, ranked tables, a month calendar) the semantics are re-skinned into Dota-native ideas (medal podium, kill split bar, hero tint caps) rather than left as stock components. An unrelated SaaS could not wear this unchanged.

**Deterministic scan**: `impeccable detect --json web/src` exited **2**, returning **4 findings (3 primary, 1 advisory)** — all in `web/src/styles.css`, all in the `slop` category:
- `border-accent-on-rounded` (warning) at `styles.css:401` and `:596` — the documented "accent cap" `border-top: 3px`.
- `gradient-text` (warning) at `styles.css:378` — the `.kpi-value` gradient text.
- `codex-grid-background` (advisory) at `styles.css:116` — the ambient grid-line background.

Three of these four are deliberate, documented design signatures, i.e. false positives *relative to product intent* (the accent cap, the ambient bed, and — arguably — the brand glow). The one I would keep on the list is `gradient-text`: it collides with the ledger's own premise that the numbers are trusted.

**Visual overlays**: The desktop browser is not connected to this session, so **no user-visible overlay exists**. Verification was instead run headlessly with Playwright, injecting `detect.js` into a fresh page. Injection preflight passed (`document.title` mutated, `<script>` appended) and the detector ran in-page on four routes:

## Overall Impression

This is the rare private dashboard that has a point of view: it reads like a record book that happens to be alive, and the numbers are set with the discipline the concept demands. The biggest opportunity is not the look — it's that a handful of the system's own signature moves (gradient KPI type, bronze medal, the soft-azure badge) actively fight legibility, and the two secondary routes don't share the front page's compositional confidence.

## What's Working

1. **The Radiant/Dire semantic pair is load-bearing, not decorative.** Splitting every two-sided comparison (win split, head-to-head, kill bars) into azure vs gold makes a dark UI instantly parseable, and the deliberate departure from in-game colours is the right call for contrast.
2. **Tabular figures + right-aligned numerics make the tables scannable.** Ranked tables, leaderboard rows and KPI values all align; the "metric is the first numeric column" rule is honoured, and the gold `pill-value` genuinely reads as "earned."
3. **Graceful degradation is designed, not bolted on.** The bounded recent-matches teaser renders nothing when the payload predates it, a missing `matches.json` becomes a single explanatory line, and errors get a plain-language card with a disclosure for the curious.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Two real WCAG-AA contrast failures on data-carrying text.**
- **What**: The rank-3 medal numeral is `#0d0d0f` on a bronze gradient (detector: 3.7:1, ×8 on `/`), and the azure "Radiant win" badge is `#2f80ed` on `#263348` (3.3:1 on `/matches`). Both are below the 4.5:1 needed for text this size (11–12px).
- **Why it matters**: Rank 3 and "who won" are exactly the facts a low-vision reader needs; they are the only two places in the system where colour is the *primary* carrier and both fail.
- **Fix**: Darken the bronze numeral's background or switch its glyph to a light ink; for the `b1` badge, lighten the azure text (or deepen the soft-fill) until it clears 4.5:1, and audit `b1` against all four surfaces it sits on.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable audit` (contrast), then `/impeccable colorize`.

**[P1] The dashboard violates its own heading outline.**
- **What**: The first heading after `<h1>Dota 2 Lobby Stats</h1>` is `<h3>Faceless Void</h3>` — the spotlight card jumps from h1 to h3 with no h2. Sections that follow (`Recent matches`) do use h2.
- **Why it matters**: A screen-reader user navigating by heading hears a level-3 before any level-2 and cannot build a correct map of the page.
- **Fix**: Promote the spotlight/eyebrow headings to real `h2`/`h3` in document order, or wrap each dashboard section (Summary, Recent, Leaderboards…) in an `h2` whose text matches the nav labels.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable audit`, then `/impeccable harden`.

**[P2] Large dead zones weaken two of the four routes.**
- **What**: On `/matches`, the month calendar is a short card in a tall column, leaving a large empty region beside the match list; on `/leaderboard/*` and `/player/*`, the page header reserves a wide right area that stays empty above the content.
- **Why it matters**: Empty space this large reads as "unfinished", and it pushes the actual content down on smaller desktops; it undercuts the dense-ledger promise.
- **Fix**: On `/matches`, make the calendar sticky or cap its column and let the list dominate; on detail pages, right-align the summary strip or reduce the head block's vertical claim.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout`.

**[P2] Mobile match header is cramped and wraps the winner badge.**
- **What**: At 390px the match card's single header row packs time + duration, the winner badge, the kill split bar and the score; "Dire win" breaks onto two lines.
- **Why it matters**: The winner is the fastest fact to read on a phone and it currently reads as a broken label; the row also crowds the thumb zone it should invite.
- **Fix**: Give the header a two-row mobile layout (time+score first, badge+bar second) and keep the badge on one line.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable adapt`.

**[P2] Gradient KPI type contradicts the ledger's own thesis.**
- **What**: The `.kpi-value` is painted white→azure→gold with `background-clip:text` (flagged by the detector both in CLI and in-page). Every other figure in the product is solid ink for a reason.
- **Why it matters**: The design explicitly argues that "the numbers are trusted, so the design does not need to sell them" — and then decorates the single most-trusted number. It is the one place the system reads as generic-generative rather than authored.
- **Fix**: Cut the gradient on the KPI figures (or reserve it for the brand mark alone) and let size/weight carry the emphasis.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable quieter` (or `/impeccable distill`).

**[P3] No in-place definitions for the terms a mixed-skill group will question.**
- **What**: "minimum sample", "KDA", "Matchups" and the win-rate threshold are never explained; `metadata.thresholds` exists in the contract but nothing surfaces the number used.
- **Fix**: One-line helper text or tooltips at the controls, and label the ambiguity ("Matchups" → "Rivals & duos").
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (impatient power user)** — The `/` search shortcut exists only in the Players table (Tables.tsx); the leaderboard detail search has neither a shortcut nor a `kbd` hint, so the same action behaves differently on two "search players" inputs. Filters, sort and minimum-sample are component state only: Alex cannot bookmark "min 3, sorted by win rate" or survive a refresh. Reaching a category means clicking a tile; there is no command palette or keyboard path between categories.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent)** — Bronze medal numeral (3.7:1) and azure winner badge (3.3:1) fail AA; the h1→h3 skip breaks heading navigation; the mini-bars (`.bar`, `.splitbar`) are `div`s with no `role`/`aria` and encode magnitude by width alone (the numeric value is adjacent in most cases, which saves it, but the recent-match kill bar is `aria-hidden` and its score is fine). The donut and hero strip correctly carry `role="img"` alternatives, and custom controls do expose `:focus-visible` — the foundation is good, the two failures are specific.

**Casey (distracted mobile user)** — The winner badge wraps on the match card; the horizontally-scrollable nav clips "Matchups" behind a fade (discoverable, but the last item is easy to miss); the leaderboard detail summary strip collapses 3-up → 2+1, leaving a ragged hole; and on `/matches` the full month calendar must be scrolled past before any match is reached. Many interactive targets measure under 44px (rank links, badges, calendar chrome).

## Minor Observations

- The accent-cap `border-top: 3px` and ambient grid/radial background are deliberate, documented signatures — the detector's `border-accent-on-rounded`, `radial-halo` and `codex-grid-background` are false positives *relative to intent*, though the "all-caps cap on every tile" density is worth a second look at scale.
- `nested-cards` fires on the inner wells (stat tiles, match cards). DESIGN.md says "panels hold content, not other cards" — the visual ramp makes this work, but the claim and the DOM disagree; consider naming inner wells explicitly so the rule isn't read as broken.
- `overused-font` (Inter 95–97%) is informational; Sora is correctly reserved for display/headline.
- The `tiny-text 11.5px` badges/tags are at the floor of comfortable reading on the leaderboard detail page.
- The sample `stats.json` carries no `metadata.thresholds`, so the "minimum sample" default shown is not sourced from the contract in this environment.

## Questions to Consider

- Should the KPI figure be the one *most* decorated number in a product whose entire thesis is trust in the numbers?
- If the bronze medal numeral can't clear 4.5:1 with dark ink, is the round medal the right form for rank 3 on a dark field — or should the podium take a rim-light instead of a fill?
- Does `/matches` need a full month grid when the group plays a handful of lobbies a month, or would a compact week strip beside the list do the same job in a third of the height?
